Acoustic impedance & Z-matching

Introduction

In this step, you'll learn how the physical property called acoustic impedance decides whether ultrasonic energy makes it across the interface between your probe and the test piece — or gets reflected at the surface before it ever reaches a defect.

Acoustic impedance governs three observable outcomes in a UT inspection:

  • Energy transfer — how much of the emitted sound actually enters the material.
  • Reflection amplitude — how strongly echoes return from discontinuities.
  • Couplant selection — which fluid bridges the probe and the surface without losing signal.

What is acoustic impedance?

Acoustic impedance (Z) is a material property equal to the product of density (ρ) and ultrasonic velocity (c):

◉ Definition · Formula Z = ρ × c   where ρ = density (kg/m³) and c = longitudinal velocity (m/s). Unit: kg/(m²·s) or Rayl.

When an ultrasonic wave meets a boundary between two materials with very different Z values, most of its energy reflects back — this is why air gaps between the probe and the specimen are catastrophic for inspection, and why a couplant is required.

Fig. 3.2a · Energy transfer vs. impedance mismatch
Without a couplant, the impedance mismatch between air and steel reflects nearly all ultrasonic energy back before it can reach a flaw. A couplant with an intermediate Z value dramatically improves transfer.

Choosing the right couplant

In field inspection, couplant choice depends on three variables: surface condition, temperature, and orientation. The rule of thumb is to match the couplant's acoustic impedance to a value roughly halfway between probe and specimen.

  1. Glycerin-based gel — most common for general steel inspection at room temperature.
  2. Cellulose paste — for vertical and overhead surfaces where gels would run.
  3. Water — in immersion setups; cheap and clean but requires a tank.
  4. High-temperature couplant — special formulations above 100 °C for weld inspection on hot pipes.
◉ Remember · Before the next step The DAC curve you'll build in Step 3.3 is only valid if the same couplant is used during both calibration and inspection. Switching couplants mid-inspection invalidates your reference.
